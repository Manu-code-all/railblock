Laptop prep

The screen footage is already captured, so none of this is needed to shoot the video. Do it anyway before the finale — this is how you demo live to a judge.

1 · The app has to run

cd "C:\Users\manug\Downloads\SIH\prototype"
streamlit run app.py

It opens at localhost:8501. Click through all three scenarios once and confirm the Gantt draws. If it errors, fix it now, not on shoot day.

2 · Clean the browser

  • Hide the bookmarks bar — Ctrl + Shift + B. Yours is full of personal links (Gmail, LinkedIn, course folders). This video is a public submission; none of that should be in it.
  • Open a fresh window with only the app tab. No other tabs — tab titles are readable in the recording.
  • Zoom to 110–125 % — Ctrl and +. Default Streamlit text is unreadable once the video is squeezed to 10 MB. This is the single highest-value setting on this page.
  • Hide extension icons if any show a badge or a personal avatar.

3 · Silence the machine

  • Windows Do Not Disturb on — a notification popping up mid-take kills it.
  • Quit WhatsApp, Discord, Slack, mail, and anything that toasts.
  • Plug in the power and set the screen never to sleep.
  • Display at 1920×1080, 100 % scaling.
  • Neutral desktop wallpaper, in case a frame catches it.

Already handled for you. The app now pins itself to the light theme and hides Streamlit's Deploy button and hamburger menu, so it looks identical on any laptop and there is no stray UI in your footage. That lives in prototype\.streamlit\config.toml — don't delete it.

Where to shoot

Audio decides whether this looks amateur, not the camera. Pick the room for sound first.

FactorDo thisNot this
Room Small room with soft things — bedroom, curtains, sofa, bed. Cloth absorbs echo. Corridor, empty classroom, lab, stairwell. Hard walls make you sound distant and hollow.
Light Window in front of you, lighting your face. Window behind you — you become a silhouette. The most common mistake in these videos.
Background Plain wall. Clear the clutter out of frame. Busy shelves, laundry, other people walking through.
Noise Fan and AC off during every take. Windows shut. Ceiling fan running — it is a constant hum you cannot remove later.
Camera Phone horizontal, on a stack of books at eye level, about an arm's length away. 1080p. Vertical video. Handheld. Looking down at a phone on the desk.
Mic Wired earbuds with the mic near your collar. Phone on airplane mode. Laptop's built-in mic. A call landing mid-take.

Shoot all three presenters in the same room, same spot, same framing. Three different rooms cut together badly and look thrown-together.

Do a 10-second audio test and actually listen back on headphones before the first real take. Every team that skips this discovers the fan hum after everyone has gone home.

Why this beat exists
Judges have sat through a hundred videos overselling a prototype. Being the team that says plainly what is and isn't built buys you more credibility than another claim would.

Shoot order

Not the order of the script. Shoot to minimise resets.

OrderWhatWhoWhy now

ThenAll of Presenter A — beats 1, 2A + crew Room and framing set once per presenter.
ThenAll of Presenter B — beat 3, plus voiceover for 4 and 5 B + crew B records the demo voiceover watching the screen pass play back.

Take plan

  • One full rehearsal out loud, timed, before anything is recorded.
  • Three takes minimum per beat. They cost seconds; a reshoot costs an evening.
  • Vary the wording each take. The best one is usually the second or third, once the nerves drop.
  • If you fluff a word and correct yourself naturally — keep it. It reads as genuine, which is the whole point.

Recording the screen

  • Windows has it built in: Win + Alt + R starts and stops. Win + G opens the full Game Bar.
  • OBS Studio is better if anyone already knows it — use Window Capture on the browser, not the whole desktop.
  • Record the browser window only. Taskbar, clock and desktop icons should not appear.
  • Record at 1080p. It gets scaled down in the export.

Edit & export

Editor owns this. Free tools: CapCut, Clipchamp (already on Windows 11), or DaVinci Resolve.

Assembly

  • Lay the beats down in order, 1 through 8.
  • Face-to-camera for beats 1, 2, 3, 7, 8. Screen recording with voice underneath for 4, 5, 6.
  • Cut on the handovers — A to B, B to C. Straight cuts, no wipes or spins.
  • Optional: a small name-and-role caption the first time each presenter appears. Nothing more.
  • Music is optional and risky. If you use any, keep it under −25 dB — the voice has to stay completely clear.
  • No AI voice, no AI avatar, no AI-generated footage.

Export settings

10 MB for 2:48 is about 400 kbps total. Plan for it rather than discovering it at the end.

SettingValueWhy
Resolution1280 × 720 At this bitrate 1080p looks worse, not better — the encoder has to throw away more.
CodecH.264 / MP4Universally accepted.
Video bitrate~300 kbpsLeaves room for audio.
AudioAAC 96 kbpsPlenty for speech.
Frame rate30 fpsMatch what you shot.

If the export overshoots

Two-pass ffmpeg targets the size far more reliably than an editor's quality slider. Run it from the folder holding your export:

ffmpeg -y -i input.mp4 -c:v libx264 -b:v 300k -vf scale=1280:720 -pass 1 -an -f mp4 NUL
ffmpeg -y -i input.mp4 -c:v libx264 -b:v 300k -vf scale=1280:720 -pass 2 -c:a aac -b:a 96k output.mp4

Still over? Drop video to 250k, or scale to 960:540. Check the file size before you upload.

Watch the finished file on a phone before uploading. That is how a judge will most likely see it. If the Gantt labels aren't readable on a phone, go back and raise the browser zoom — that is the fix, not more bitrate.

What sinks these videos

  • Reading a script. Obvious on camera, and it's the one thing the brief explicitly prohibits.
  • Ninety seconds on the problem, twenty on the solution. Flip it — the working demo is the reason you're ahead.
  • Slides only, when you have working software. Most teams at this stage have nothing to show. You do. Show it.
  • Backlit faces and echoey rooms. Fixable in thirty seconds beforehand, impossible afterwards.
  • Claiming things that aren't built. Beat 8 exists to stop this. One overclaim a judge catches costs more than the claim gained.
  • Running over three minutes. Cut a point rather than talking faster.
  • Unreadable screen text. Browser zoom. Every time.
